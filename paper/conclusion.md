# 6. Conclusion

Online adaptation turns operational decisions into inputs to the future authentication state. This study characterized one accepted-only feedback loop with two coupled effects: malicious inclusion when accepted impostor observations alter the profile, and genuine-evidence censoring when rejected claimant observations are excluded from adaptation. The evaluation combined a controlled HMOG mechanism study with an independent two-day McGill directional replication rather than treating poisoning security and adaptation utility as separate tasks.

At the historical reference operating points, Blind EMA showed large positive post-poison ASR amplification under the score-informed boundary benchmark. At \(K=8\), HMOG ASR increased from 22.92% for Frozen to 53.96% for Blind EMA; the paired increase was +31.04 percentage points (95% CI [+26.95, +35.20]). Guarded EMA retained the access rule while imposing a separate update-authorization condition and returned ASR to 22.93%; the reference-point direction was reproduced on McGill. The prospectively specified operating-point sensitivity analysis materially bounded this conclusion. Amplification was much smaller at nominal 5%, and the mean Blind-minus-Frozen effect at nominal 1% was −0.447 points on HMOG and −0.354 points on McGill in the tested boundary scenario. On the same HMOG nominal-1% cohort, however, Blind EMA increased FRR by 6.89 points and HTER by 3.22 points. Lower ASR therefore did not imply harmless updating or general poisoning robustness.

The operating-point results also limited the scope of the original Guarded rule. Its enrollment-anchored effective margin is zero whenever the authentication threshold reaches or exceeds the enrollment mean; this state was common after strict recalibration. Recalibration changed \(\tau_0\), the associated claimant-specific \(k\), selected boundary candidates, and the \(K=8\)-feasible cohort, so the observed reversal cannot be attributed to changing \(\tau_0\) alone. Nominal calibration FAR was not a held-out guarantee, and the strict conditions had high genuine rejection. The boundary strategy assumed score access, while \(K\) excluded screening and reconnaissance; a binary-feedback-only attack was not demonstrated.

The clean temporal experiments exposed the complementary adaptation cost. During the controlled HMOG claimant-only sensitivity shift, accepted-only policies excluded rejected genuine observations from learning and retained a higher-scoring subset. Guarded profile trajectories therefore did not follow the full genuine-score movement, and Guarded did not preserve positive late-FRR utility relative to Frozen. The label-aware Oracle-all information-availability intervention restored every genuine adaptation observation and changed the profile trajectory in the downward direction. In claimant-only HMOG at \(D=2\), it lowered late FRR by 6.60 percentage points relative to Frozen, but late FAR increased by 6.08 points. The Oracle effect was also present at \(D=0\), so it partly reflects operating-point movement rather than recovery uniquely caused by synthetic drift. Oracle-all is non-deployable and not a formal upper bound; it supports accepted-only feedback censoring as a contributor to weak recovery, not a uniquely correct adaptation path.

The prespecified recovery/state probes did not identify a deployable resolution within their frozen designs. TQ-EMA v1 did not meet its late-FRR recovery criterion. TQ-EMA v2 and the Adaptive Quantile Profile did not satisfy their validation gates, so outer test scores were not loaded and no outer estimates exist for those methods. These stopped branches are supporting negative evidence rather than headline algorithmic contributions. The McGill experiment reproduced the directions of the boundary-poisoning, update-authorization, and Oracle feedback effects under a different classifier, accelerometer-only inputs, and real Day-1/Day-2 separation. That cross-day shift can combine behavior, context, placement, acquisition, and model-transfer effects; it is a small mechanism-level replication, not isolated natural-drift measurement or benchmark validation.

The main implication is that access permission and learning permission should be treated as distinct design decisions, but a nominally separate gate is not necessarily active throughout the reachable state space. Adaptive authentication should report contamination, post-update ASR, genuine rejection, calibration transfer, gate activity, and attack feasibility jointly across explicit operating points. Practical recovery may require trusted evidence that is not determined solely by the current access decision, together with bounded state movement. Larger untouched multi-day studies should evaluate such evidence under benign temporal change and adversarial contamination in the same frozen protocol. The present findings define this joint evaluation problem; they do not claim a universal solution or an impossibility result.
