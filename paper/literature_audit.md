# Literature Audit

Audit date: 3 October 2026.

This is a targeted reference audit for Paper 3, not a new systematic review. Candidate works were discovered through topic- and title-based searches covering continuous mobile authentication, adaptive biometric template updating, poisoning, selective feedback, concept drift, security--usability trade-offs, and recovery mechanisms. Bibliographic metadata was then checked against DOI records, publisher pages, official proceedings, or official dataset pages. Paper 1 and Paper 2 were taken from `paper/paper1_paper2_fact_check.md`. A source is included only when its identity and the claim for which it will be used could be verified. General machine-learning papers are explicitly marked as analogous when they do not study biometric authentication.

## Audit Conclusions

- The literature directly supports the need to distinguish authentication decisions from template-update decisions, but the two-threshold idea itself predates Paper 3 (`Giot2011KeystrokeUpdate`).
- Direct biometric poisoning evidence predates Paper 3 in adaptive face and fingerprint systems (`Biggio2012PoisoningAdaptive`, `Biggio2013FacePoisoning`, `Lovisotto2020BiometricBackdoors`, `Xue2020LOPA`). Paper 3 must therefore claim neither the first biometric poisoning attack nor invention of guarded updating.
- The strongest defensible positioning is the joint, protocol-controlled evaluation of held-out poisoning, update authorization, legitimate temporal recovery, accepted-only feedback censoring, fail-closed negative branches, and independent two-day replication in mobile behavioral authentication.
- Selective labels, self-training confirmation bias, and performative prediction provide **analogous theoretical context**. They are not direct evidence for the exact authentication mechanism, and “selective-update bias caused by accepted-only feedback censoring” is Paper 3's scoped term.
- No audited source materially eliminates the Paper 3 positioning above. The prior work materially narrows novelty at the component level, which is reflected in the Introduction and Related Work.
