Behavioral continuous authentication can update a user profile during operation, but accepted-only adaptation couples two risks: accepted impostor observations can alter future profile state, whereas rejected genuine observations are excluded from learning. We characterize this malicious-inclusion/genuine-censoring mechanism in score-level online authentication. The study used frozen HMOG scores for held-out random and score-informed boundary poisoning, controlled claimant-only sensitivity shifts, and matched Frozen, Blind exponential moving-average (EMA), Guarded EMA, and label-aware Oracle-all controls. Reference-point findings were replicated on the two-day McGill Gait dataset. A prospectively specified operating-point sensitivity analysis on the score caches evaluated reference, nominal-5%, and nominal-1% calibration-FAR conditions.

At the reference operating points, boundary poisoning at \(K=8\) increased post-poison attack success rate (ASR) from 22.92% to 53.96% on HMOG and from 12.37% to 32.09% on McGill; Guarded EMA returned ASR close to the corresponding Frozen values. This direction was not invariant under recalibration. At the nominal-1% condition, the mean Blind-minus-Frozen boundary effect was −0.447 percentage points on HMOG and −0.354 points on McGill. Full-cohort Frozen false rejection rate (FRR) was then 80.79% and 90.25%, respectively. On the same HMOG boundary cohort, Blind EMA reduced ASR by 0.447 points but increased FRR by 6.89 points and HTER by 3.22 points relative to Frozen. Under claimant-only \(D=2\), Oracle-all lowered late FRR by 6.60 points but increased late FAR by 6.08 points.

Thus, accepted impostors can modify adaptive state, but the magnitude and direction of the resulting ASR effect depend on the recalibrated policy and feasible attack paths. The enrollment-anchored Guarded rule was strongly restrictive at the reference points, but its additional effective margin often vanished after stricter calibration. Nominal FAR constraints were not held-out guarantees, and lower post-poison ASR did not imply harmless adaptation. Poisoning, genuine rejection, gate activity, and attack feasibility must therefore be reported jointly across explicit operating points.
